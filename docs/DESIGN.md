# ReelTide Design Direction

## Art Direction

ReelTide should feel like a mature premium media product: quiet, precise, tactile, cinematic, and content-first.

The Liquid Glass direction is inspired by the principles of modern Apple interfaces—layering, translucency, depth, responsive material, and fluid motion—not by copying proprietary screens or blindly reproducing effects.

## Taste Rules

### Prefer

- strong composition;
- generous but purposeful spacing;
- restrained monochrome contrast;
- clear typographic hierarchy;
- subtle material depth;
- optical alignment;
- consistent component geometry;
- content-led composition;
- motion with purpose;
- small details that reinforce one coherent system.

### Reject

- generic Material-card grids;
- arbitrary gradients;
- excessive glass blur;
- rainbow/neon accents without product meaning;
- oversized floating controls;
- decorative noise;
- inconsistent corner radii;
- excessive shadows;
- animation for animation's sake;
- visual tricks that reduce readability;
- isolated “pretty” screens that do not belong to the same product.

## Composition

Treat the screen as a hierarchy of layers: content, atmospheric/background layer, material controls, and transient interaction layers. Every layer needs a reason to exist.

Whitespace is a structural tool. Do not fill empty space simply because it is available.

## Material and Lighting

Glass should suggest a physical surface interacting with content beneath it. Highlights, borders, blur, and opacity must be subtle and related to the surface's hierarchy.

Use fewer, stronger material levels rather than many effects.

## Typography

Typography should feel native, modern, and editorial. Titles should have authority; metadata should recede; controls should be compact and legible.

Avoid excessive font weights and decorative typography.

## Motion Direction

Motion should feel physical rather than theatrical. Use spring behavior for direct manipulation and short, purposeful transitions for state changes. Preserve continuity between related surfaces.

## Visual Review Questions

Before accepting a screen, ask:

1. What is the user's primary action?
2. What is the visual focal point?
3. Is the hierarchy obvious without color?
4. Does every glass layer improve hierarchy?
5. Are controls discoverable?
6. Does the screen belong to the same design system as the rest of the app?
7. Does the design remain good with real content, long titles, missing artwork, and loading/error states?
8. Does the effect justify its rendering cost?
9. Is there any generic/template/AI-looking artifact that should be removed?
10. Would a skilled product designer consider this finished rather than merely styled?

## Excellence Standard

Do not implement the first visually acceptable solution. Compare alternatives mentally, choose the strongest composition, and refine hierarchy, spacing, material, typography, and motion together.

Consistency is more important than novelty. Restraint is more important than adding effects.
